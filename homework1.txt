1. 直接回答问题（特殊疑问句-回答特殊疑问词，一般疑问句-yes/no，选择疑问句-二选其一/neither）
2. 过去发生的具体事件（自己的真实经历、别人的意见建议、社会的共识、谚语）
3. 自己现在的感受、思考、态度 
4. 总结

连接词：
2、3句之间：
正面影响-so, therefore, thus, as a result, consequently
转折让步-despite, in spite of, although, even if

第4句：
总而言之-all in all, overall, in conclusion, to sum up, in summary
简而言之-in brief, in short, in a word, briefly speaking

2-The area you live in

It's a university area, so most people are students or staff, which makes the vibe pretty relaxed.

Q:Do you like the area that you live in?
A:Yes, I do. I live near  university town, and it's convenient with shops, cafes, and bus stops everywhere. When I first moved here , 
I thought it was too far from downtown, but now I actually prefer the quieter pace. 

Q:Where do you like to go in that area?
A:There's a small park behind our campus with a pond and walking paths. I go there when I need to clear my head before exams.
 Sometimes I bring my laptop and study under the trees. It's my little escape spot.

Q:Do you know any famous people in your area?
A:Not really. I mean, there are some professors who are well-known in their fields, but they're not celebrity-famous. 
Once I saw a local news reporter filming near the campus gate, but that's about it. Pretty ordinary, honestly.

Q:What have some recent changes in the area been?
A:A new subway line opened last year, which made getting to the city center way easier. Also, a bunch of new cafes and co-working spaces popped up around here,
 probably because more students are freelancing now. The area feels more modern than it did two years ago.

Q:Do you know any of your neighbors?
A:Yeah, the students in my building basically. We don't hang out much, but we nod at each other in the hallway and sometimes share snacks during exam season. 
My neighbour knows more people because he's more outgoing.


3-Hometown
Q:Where do you come from?
A:I come from Xuzhou, located in the north of Jiangsu Province.It is a ancient city with a history of over 2600 years and is known as the birthplace of Han culture.

Q:What's the most attractive part of your hometown?
A:The food, definitely. Xuzhou barbecue is famous for a reason—grilled lamb skewers with cumin and chili, eaten late at night with friends, it's just unbeatable. 
My friends and I used to hit up the same stall near our apartment almost every weekend in high school.

Q:Has your hometown changed much these years?
A:Yes, a lot. New subway lines have opened, and there are shopping malls everywhere now. The old neighborhoods I remember from childhood are 
being replaced by high-rises. It's exciting to see the city grow, but I also miss the quieter streets from before.

Q:Do you think you will live in this place in the future?
A:Probably yes. My parents are there, and the cost of living is way lower than in bigger cities like Shanghai or Hangzhou. 
After graduation, I'm considering looking for a job nearby so I can be closer to them. 


4-Accommodation
Q:Do you live in a house or a flat?
A:I live in a flat. My parents and I have an apartment in downtown Xuzhou, on one of the higher floors.
 We've been there for about ten years now, and it's comfortable enough for our family.

Q:Do you like your living environment?
A:Yes, I do. Being in the city center means everything is close—supermarkets, restaurants, the subway. My mom especially loves it because 
she can walk to the market every morning. The only downside is it gets a bit noisy during rush hour, but I've gotten used to it. In brief, it's urban and  lively.

Q:What's your favorite house?
A:A villa with a private garden.I remember visiting my aunt's place in the suburbs a few years ago. She had a small garden with flowers and 
even a little vegetable patch. Sitting on her balcony in the evening, watching the sunset, felt so peaceful compared to the city.
 I think that's the kind of space that stays with you.

Q:What kind of house or flat do you want to live in?
A:Somewhere with a balcony at least, maybe a small garden if I'm lucky. I don't need anything fancy, but I'd love to have a quiet corner 
where I can read or just sit and think. After living in a dorm and then a busy city apartment, I crave that kind of calm.

27-Food and cooking
Q:What is your favorite food?
A:Buddha Jumps Over the Wall. My roommate took me to try it at a restaurant in Fuzhou for my 19th birthday, and I still remember the rich broth 
and all those ingredients layered together.It's expensive so I don't eat it often, but whenever I do, it feels like a celebration. 

Q:Is there any food you dislike?
A:Yes, bitter melon. My grandma used to force me to eat it when I was little, saying it's good for health, but I just couldn't get past the bitterness. 
Even now I avoid it on menus. I know it's nutritious, but some things just don't work for me.

Q:What kind of new food would you like to try?
A:Authentic Thai curry. I've only had the takeout version from a chain restaurant, but one of my classmates went to Bangkok last year and
 kept raving about how different the real thing is. I'd love to experience that firsthand.

Q:Who normally does the cooking in your home?
A:My mom. She's been the main cook since I was born, and she actually enjoys it. My dad helps with grocery shopping and cleaning up, 
but the actual cooking is her territory. She says feeding the family is her way of showing love. 

Q:In general, do you prefer eating out or eating at home?
A:Eating at home. My mom's cooking just hits different, you know? Plus, eating out with friends can get expensive, and as a student, 
I'm always watching my budget. There's something cozy about sitting around the dinner table at home too.
